This guide is applicable to all types of sports venues from community recreation centers to professional stadiums.

## 1. ADA COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS

### 1.1 General Requirements

Sports facilities must comply with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and provide accessible routes, doors, assembly sections, and toilet facilities. All newly constructed and altered facilities must meet current ADA standards.

#### Key Compliance Areas:
- Accessible routes throughout facility
- Wheelchair seating locations
- Companion seating
- Accessible entrances
- Accessible parking spaces
- Locker rooms and restrooms
- Common use circulation paths
- Assembly areas

### 1.2 Accessible Elements Required

Sports facilities must provide accessible routes connecting all accessible elements and spaces within areas of indoor or outdoor sports activities, including:
- Playing surfaces and courts
- Team and player seating areas
- Participant support areas (locker rooms, training rooms)
- Spectator seating areas
- Food service and concession areas
- Retail shops and pro shops
- Administrative offices

### 1.3 Primary Source Documents

- **2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design**
- **U.S. Access Board Chapter 10: Sports Facilities**
- **ADAAG (Americans with Disabilities Act Accessibility Guidelines)**
- State and local accessibility codes (may be more stringent)

## 2. UNIVERSAL DESIGN PRINCIPLES

### 2.1 Seven Principles of Universal Design

#### Principle 1: Equitable Use
- Design is useful and marketable to people with diverse abilities
- Provide same means of use for all users (identical whenever possible, equivalent when not)
- Avoid segregating or stigmatizing any users
- Provisions for privacy, security, and safety equally available to all

#### Principle 2: Flexibility in Use
- Design accommodates a wide range of individual preferences and abilities
- Provide choice in methods of use
- Accommodate right- or left-handed access and use
- Facilitate user's accuracy and precision
- Provide adaptability to user's pace

#### Principle 3: Simple and Intuitive Use
- Use of design is easy to understand
- Eliminate unnecessary complexity
- Accommodate a wide range of literacy and language skills
- Arrange information consistent with its importance
- Provide effective prompting and feedback

#### Principle 4: Perceptible Information
- Design communicates necessary information effectively
- Use different modes for redundant presentation of essential information
- Provide adequate contrast between essential information and surroundings
- Maximize legibility of essential information
- Provide compatibility with assistive technology

#### Principle 5: Tolerance for Error
- Design minimizes hazards and adverse consequences of accidents
- Arrange elements to minimize hazards and errors
- Provide warnings of hazards and errors
- Provide fail-safe features
- Discourage unconscious action in tasks requiring vigilance

#### Principle 6: Low Physical Effort
- Design can be used efficiently and comfortably with minimum fatigue
- Allow user to maintain neutral body position
- Use reasonable operating forces
- Minimize repetitive actions
- Minimize sustained physical effort

#### Principle 7: Size and Space for Approach and Use
- Appropriate size and space provided for approach, reach, manipulation, and use
- Provide clear line of sight to important elements
- Make reach to all components comfortable for seated or standing users
- Accommodate variations in hand and grip size
- Provide adequate space for use of assistive devices

### 2.2 Application to Sports Venues

#### Accessible Spectator Facilities
- Choice of accessible vantage points
- Opportunity to sit with companion or within larger group
- Modern stadiums feature sensory rooms, augmented hearing systems, wheelchair-accessible seating
- Accessible viewing platforms giving people true choice rather than limiting them to designated areas

#### Pathway Design
- Leading to entrance and on escape routes: minimum 2000mm (78.7") wide
- Pathway surfaces: firm, slip-resistant, and smooth
- Special attention to emergency access and evacuation routes
- Elevator capacity for wheelchairs
- Proper bathroom dimensions

#### Inclusive Environment
- Design venues with universal accessibility features
- Ramps, elevators, and accessible seating promote inclusivity
- Enable spectators of all abilities to enjoy sporting events
- Design products and environments usable by all people to greatest extent possible without adaptation or specialized design

## 3. ACCESSIBLE SEATING AND VIEWING AREAS

### 3.1 Wheelchair Seating Requirements

#### Number of Wheelchair Spaces Required
- **Minimum:** At least 1% of total seating must be wheelchair seating locations
- **Distribution:** Must be horizontally and vertically dispersed throughout all seating areas
- **Integration:** Integral part of seating plan so wheelchair users not isolated
- **All Areas:** Must be provided in all areas including sky boxes and specialty areas
- **Price Ranges:** Must provide choice of admission prices comparable to general public

#### Wheelchair Space Specifications
- **Definition:** Open, level space that accommodates one person using wheelchair
- **Surface:** Smooth, stable, and slip-resistant
- **View:** Provide views comparable to those for general public
- **Flexible Use:** Removable or folding seats can be provided for use when not occupied by wheelchair users

### 3.2 Companion Seating

#### Requirements
- **Quantity:** One companion seat must be provided next to each wheelchair seating location
- **Type:** Conventional seat that accommodates friend or companion
- **Purpose:** Ensures wheelchair users can sit with family and friends

### 3.3 Sightlines

#### Standing Events
- In assembly areas where spectators expected to stand during event
- Wheelchair seating locations must enable people to see over heads of spectators standing in front
- Applies to arenas, stadiums for concerts, sporting events with standing spectators

#### Unobstructed Views
- Every wheelchair seating location must have unobstructed view of playing area
- Sightline calculations critical in design phase
- Consider elevation, rake angle, and proximity to activity

### 3.4 Multiple Accessible Seating Options

#### Indoor Arenas and Stadiums Must Provide:
- Comprehensive accessibility features
- Multiple accessible seating options throughout venue
- Companion seating with unobstructed views
- Availability in all price categories
- Distribution across all levels and sections

## 5. ACCESSIBLE LOCKER ROOMS AND RESTROOMS

### 5.1 Locker Room Requirements

#### General Requirements
- When dressing, fitting, or locker rooms provided, they must be accessible
- Must comply with all ADAAG provisions
- If in a cluster, 5% or at least one must be accessible
- Accessible route through door and to all required elements

#### Locker Specifications
- **Quantity:** At least 5% but not less than one of each type must be accessible
- **Types:** Full-size, half-size, quarter-size, etc.
- **Clear Floor Space:** Positioned for approach by wheelchair users
- **Hardware:** Operable with one hand, no tight grasping, pinching, or twisting

### 5.2 Bench Requirements

#### Accessible Bench Specifications
- **Required:** In all dressing, fitting, and locker rooms
- **Seat Depth:** Minimum 20 inches to maximum 24 inches
- **Seat Length:** 42 inches minimum
- **Seat Height:** 17 to 19 inches above finished floor
- **Clear Floor Space:** Positioned to allow approach parallel to short end of bench
- **Back Support:** Fixed or folding seat back where walls not available

### 5.3 Restroom Requirements

#### General Accessibility
- Restrooms must be accessible to everyone
- Facilities such as grab bars, lowered sinks, adequate space for maneuvering wheelchairs
- At least one accessible stall in each restroom
- Accessible stalls equipped with grab bars and space for wheelchair to turn

#### Accessible Stall Specifications
- **Minimum Width:** 60 inches
- **Minimum Depth:** 56 inches for wall-hung toilet, 59 inches for floor-mounted
- **Door Swing:** Must not swing into minimum clear floor space
- **Grab Bars:** Side wall and rear wall grab bars required
- **Toilet Height:** 17-19 inches to top of seat

#### Lavatory Requirements
- **Knee Clearance:** 27 inches high minimum, 30 inches wide minimum
- **Toe Clearance:** 9 inches high minimum
- **Counter Height:** 34 inches maximum
- **Faucet Controls:** Operable with one hand, lever or automatic preferred
- **Clear Floor Space:** 30 × 48 inches minimum

### 5.4 Employee Facilities

Spaces used by employees for purposes other than work must be fully accessible:
- Restrooms
- Break rooms
- Locker rooms
- Lounges
- Employee parking

## 6. ACCESSIBLE ROUTES AND CIRCULATION

### 6.1 Accessible Route Requirements

#### Width Requirements
- **Minimum Clear Width:** 36 inches (91.44 cm) continuous
- **Passing Space:** 60 × 60 inches at intervals not exceeding 200 feet
- **Pathways to Entrance and Escape Routes:** 2000mm (78.7") wide minimum

#### Surface Requirements
- **Type:** Firm, stable, and slip-resistant
- **Changes in Level:** Maximum 1/2 inch vertical, 1/4 inch without edge treatment
- **Openings:** Gaps in walking surfaces shall not allow passage of 1/2 inch diameter sphere
- **Gratings:** Elongated openings perpendicular to dominant direction of travel

### 6.2 Ramp Requirements

#### Slope Specifications
- **Maximum Running Slope:** 1:12 (8.33%)
- **Maximum Cross Slope:** 1:48 (2.08%)
- **Walking Surfaces:** Running slope not steeper than 1:20
- **Exception for Recreation Facilities:** Where structurally or operationally infeasible, slope may not exceed 1:8

#### Rise and Run
- **Maximum Rise per Run:** 30 inches (762 mm)
- **No Limit:** On number of runs
- **Minimum Clear Width:** 36 inches (914 mm)

#### Landing Requirements
- **Landing Length:** 60 inches (1525 mm) minimum
- **Direction Changes:** Landing must be 60 × 60 inches minimum where ramps change direction
- **Door Landings:** Must not have slope steeper than 1:48
- **Top and Bottom:** Level landing at top and bottom of each ramp run

#### Handrails
- **Required:** Both sides if rise greater than 6 inches or length greater than 72 inches
- **Height:** 34-38 inches above ramp surface
- **Extension:** 12 inches minimum beyond top and bottom of ramp runs
- **Gripping Surface:** Circular cross section with outside diameter 1.25-2 inches

### 6.3 Elevator Requirements

#### Where Required
- Multi-story sports facilities must provide accessible vertical access
- Elevators required to connect all levels
- Platform lifts permitted in limited applications

#### Elevator Specifications
- **Clear Floor Space:** 51 inches minimum × 68 inches minimum for standard elevator
- **Door Clear Width:** 36 inches minimum
- **Door Timing:** Minimum 3 seconds open time
- **Controls:** 48 inches maximum height, tactile and visual indicators
- **Emergency Communications:** Visual and audible signals

## 7. ACCESSIBLE PARKING REQUIREMENTS

### 7.1 Number of Accessible Spaces

#### Calculation Table
| Total Parking Spaces | Minimum Accessible Spaces Required |
|---------------------|-----------------------------------|
| 1-25 | 1 |
| 26-50 | 2 |
| 51-75 | 3 |
| 76-100 | 4 |
| 101-150 | 5 |
| 151-200 | 6 |
| 201-300 | 7 |
| 301-400 | 8 |
| 401-500 | 9 |
| 501-1,000 | 2% of total |
| 1,001 and over | 20 plus 1 for each 100 over 1,000 |

### 7.2 Van-Accessible Spaces

#### Requirements
- **Minimum:** At least one of every six accessible parking spaces must be van-accessible
- **Calculation:** Round up to nearest whole number (e.g., 4 accessible = 1 van-accessible)

### 7.3 Space Dimensions

#### Standard Accessible Space
- **Width:** 8 feet (96 inches)
- **Access Aisle:** 5 feet (60 inches) wide
- **Total Width:** 13 feet minimum

#### Van-Accessible Space
- **Width:** 11 feet (132 inches)
- **Access Aisle:** 5 feet (60 inches) wide
- **Total Width:** 16 feet minimum
- **Alternative:** 8-foot space with 8-foot access aisle

#### Access Aisle Requirements
- **Markings:** Clearly marked to discourage parking
- **Surface:** Same level as parking space, stable, firm, slip-resistant
- **Shared:** May be shared between two accessible spaces

### 7.4 Location Requirements

#### Proximity
- Located on shortest accessible route to accessible facility entrance
- Multiple parking facilities: accessible spaces distributed among facilities
- For sports stadiums: accessible spaces should be in lot adjacent to stadium when possible

#### Example Calculation
For a sports stadium with:
- Adjacent lot: 1,000 spaces = 20 accessible (4 van-accessible)
- Remote lot: 1,500 spaces = 25 accessible (5 van-accessible)
- These 45 accessible spaces can be located in adjacent lot since it provides shortest route

### 7.5 Signage Requirements

#### Identification Signs
- **Symbol:** International symbol of accessibility
- **Mounting Height:** 60 inches minimum above ground to bottom of sign
- **Van-Accessible:** Additional sign stating "Van Accessible"
- **Visibility:** Signs cannot be obscured by parked vehicles

## 8. SAFETY REGULATIONS BY SPORT

### 8.1 OSHA Requirements for Sports Facilities

#### General Jurisdiction
- Professional sports teams and facilities must comply with OSHA requirements
- Obligation extends to college, university, and school-level facilities
- Applies to employee safety (not spectator safety in broad sense)
- Facilities with more than 10 employees required to keep records of work-related injuries

#### Key Compliance Areas

**Bloodborne Pathogens Standards**
- Exposure Control Plans required
- Proper handling of blood and bodily fluids
- Training for staff in contact with athletes
- Sharps disposal containers
- Personal protective equipment (PPE)

**Hazard Communication Standard**
- Chemicals used in facilities must be properly labeled
- Safety Data Sheets (SDS) readily available
- Employee training on chemical hazards
- Applies to cleaning products, maintenance chemicals, field treatments

**Emergency Preparedness**
- Properly marked exits
- Fire safety measures
- Emergency action plans
- Evacuation procedures

**Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)**
- Appropriate PPE for maintenance staff
- Equipment for custodial and cleaning personnel
- Sport-specific protection for trainers and medical staff

#### Record Keeping Requirements
- Work-related injuries and illnesses must be documented
- Records kept at worksite for minimum five years
- OSHA 300 Log required for facilities with 10+ employees

### 8.2 ASTM Standards for Sports Equipment and Surfaces

#### Overview
ASTM International develops safety standards for sports equipment, playing surfaces, and facilities. These are compiled in Volume 15.07 of the Annual Book of ASTM Standards.

#### Committee F08 Standards
Committee F08 on Sports Equipment, Playing Surfaces, and Facilities develops standards for:

**Playground Equipment and Surfaces**
- ASTM F1487: Consumer Safety Performance Specification for Playground Equipment for Public Use
- ASTM F2373: Public Use Play Equipment for Children 6-23 Months
- Surfaces must meet g-max and HIC (Head Injury Criterion) requirements
- CPSC Handbook for Playground Safety compliance

**Indoor Sports Facilities**
- ASTM F2440: Indoor Wall/Feature Padding specification
- Ensures safety of padding on walls and facility features
- Wrestling mat standards
- Basketball court specifications

**Athletic Fields**
- Standards for artificial turf systems
- Field performance and safety criteria
- Surface testing for shock absorption
- Traction and stability requirements

**Surface Testing Requirements**
Sports and playground surfaces must be tested for:
- Impact attenuation (g-max testing)
- Head Injury Criterion (HIC) values
- Slip resistance
- Stability
- Drainage

### 8.3 Sport-Specific Safety Standards

#### Field Sports (Football, Soccer, Lacrosse)
- Goal post padding requirements
- Sideline buffer zones
- Proper field drainage
- Surface evenness and stability
- Spectator barriers

#### Court Sports (Basketball, Volleyball, Tennis)
- Court surface slip resistance
- Proper line markings
- Clearance zones around courts
- Lighting requirements
- Net and equipment standards

#### Ice Sports (Hockey, Figure Skating)
- Dasher board specifications (42" height)
- Protective glass/acrylic (8' minimum at ends)
- Ice temperature and thickness standards
- Refrigeration safety systems
- Zamboni access and storage

#### Aquatic Facilities (Swimming, Diving, Water Polo)
- Water quality standards
- Depth markings and safety lines
- Diving board and platform specifications
- Pool deck slip resistance
- Lifeguard stations and equipment

## 9. EMERGENCY EGRESS REQUIREMENTS

### 9.1 Exit Requirements for Assembly Occupancies

#### Number of Exits
- **Areas serving >1,000 people:** Minimum of four remote exits required (NFPA 101)
- **Purpose:** Minimize impact of one or more exits becoming obstructed in fire emergency
- **Separation:** Exits must be remotely located from each other

#### Exit Capacity and Occupant Load
- **IBC Requirements:** Number and size of exits based on seating capacity
- **Calculations:** Must determine maximum number of occupants facility can accommodate
- **Factors:** Used to determine number of exits, width of exit doors, total exit width required

### 9.2 Exit Width and Travel Distance

#### Exit Width Requirements
- Based on occupant load and occupancy type
- Assembly occupancies: typically 0.2 inches per occupant for level egress
- Stairs: 0.3 inches per occupant

#### Travel Distance Limitations
- **Maximum Exit Access Travel in Assembly:**
  - **With NFPA 13/13R Sprinklers:** 250 feet
  - **Without Sprinklers:** 200 feet
- **Common Path of Egress Travel:** 75 feet or less in Assembly occupancies

### 9.3 Smoke-Protected Seating

#### Design Strategy
- Incorporates smoke control systems
- Extends exiting duration requirements
- Reduces number, distances, or widths of exit paths
- Cost-effective alternative to additional exits in large venues

#### System Components
- Smoke exhaust systems
- Air supply systems
- Smoke barriers
- Pressurization systems

### 9.4 Stadium-Specific Requirements

#### Safe Dispersal Areas
- Horizontal sliding or swinging gates permitted in fences surrounding stadiums
- Panic hardware not required on stadium gates under constant supervision while public present
- Safe dispersal areas: 3 square feet per occupant between fence and enclosed space

#### Emergency Voice/Alarm Systems
- Approved voice alarm system must notify occupants of hazard
- Visual alarms required in addition to audible
- Must reach all areas of facility
- Zone control for specific area evacuation

### 9.5 Emergency Lighting

#### Requirements
- **Purpose:** Illuminate egress paths, exit doors, and exit signs during power failure
- **Duration:** IBC specifies minimum duration (typically 90 minutes)
- **Intensity:** Minimum illumination levels on walking surfaces
- **Coverage:** All means of egress including stairs, corridors, aisles, passageways

#### Illumination Levels
- **Egress Paths:** 1 footcandle (10.76 lux) average, 0.1 footcandle minimum
- **Exit Discharge:** Adequate lighting to safely exit building
- **Testing:** Emergency lighting must be tested monthly and annually

### 9.6 Exit Signage

#### Requirements
- Illuminated or photoluminescent exit signs
- Visible from all directions of egress travel
- Letters minimum 6 inches high with 3/4 inch stroke width
- Red or green letters on contrasting background
- "EXIT" in plainly legible letters

## 10. FIRE SAFETY SYSTEMS

### 10.1 Fire Alarm Systems

#### General Requirements for Sports Facilities
- Manual fire alarm boxes at exits
- Automatic detection systems
- Notification appliances (audible and visual)
- Emergency voice/alarm communication systems
- Fire alarm control panel (FACP)

#### Zoning for Large Venues
- Fire alarms designed with various zones
- Pinpoints which areas need immediate evacuation
- Critical for stadiums and large arenas
- Enables phased evacuation if necessary

#### Integration Features
- Often integrated with video surveillance
- Connected to building management systems
- Integrated with emergency lighting
- Direct link to emergency responders in some systems

#### Stadium-Specific Requirements
- Must cover extensive areas and large spaces
- Communicate effectively with everyone in building
- Emergency voice/alarm systems provide clear instructions
- Both visible and audible alarms for accessibility

### 10.2 Fire Sprinkler Systems

#### Automatic Sprinkler Requirements
- Generally required in Assembly Group A occupancies
- Coverage of all areas including:
  - Seating sections
  - Concourses
  - Kitchens and concession areas
  - Storage rooms
  - Mechanical spaces
  - Locker rooms

#### System Types

**Wet Pipe Systems**
- Most common for indoor sports facilities
- Pipes constantly filled with water
- Fastest response time
- Suitable for heated spaces

**Pre-Action Systems**
- Used in higher-risk zones (electrical rooms, server rooms)
- Requires two triggers before water discharge
- Prevents false activation
- Protects sensitive equipment

**Specialized Sprinklers**
- Kitchen hood suppression systems
- High-piled storage systems
- Early suppression fast response (ESFR) for warehouses

#### Water Supply Requirements
- Stadiums equipped with high-capacity water reservoirs
- Fire pumps ensure adequate water pressure across venue
- Municipal water supply coordination
- Backflow prevention devices

#### Coordination with Fire Alarms
- Sprinkler flow switches trigger fire alarms
- Alert people and activate suppression simultaneously
- Supervisory signals monitor system integrity
- Tamper switches on valves

### 10.3 Fire Suppression in Specific Areas

#### Kitchen and Concession Areas
- Class K fire suppression systems for commercial cooking
- Automatic hood suppression
- Manual pull stations
- Gas/electric shutoff upon activation

#### Electrical and Mechanical Rooms
- Clean agent systems (FM-200, Novec 1230)
- Pre-action sprinkler systems
- Early warning smoke detection
- Protection of critical infrastructure

#### Storage Areas
- High-piled storage sprinkler requirements
- Proper clearances from sprinkler heads
- Commodity classification considerations

### 10.4 Fire Department Coordination

#### Fire Department Connections (FDC)
- Accessible locations on building exterior
- Within 100 feet of fire hydrant
- Clear signage and markings
- Regular testing and inspection

#### Fire Department Access
- Fire lanes maintained around facility
- Knox boxes for building access
- Pre-incident planning with local fire departments
- Direct alarm notification to fire department

#### Stadium Coordination Plans
- Specific coordination with local emergency services
- Some systems directly linked to emergency responders
- Regular emergency drills
- Incident command protocols

### 10.5 Inspection and Maintenance

#### Fire Alarm Systems
- Monthly testing of notification appliances
- Annual comprehensive testing
- Battery backup testing
- Documentation of all tests

#### Fire Sprinkler Systems
- Weekly/monthly inspections of valves and gauges
- Quarterly alarm testing
- Annual comprehensive inspection
- 5-year internal pipe inspection
- Obstruction investigation every 10 years

## 11. FIRST AID AND MEDICAL FACILITIES

### 11.1 Medical Facility Requirements

#### First Aid Rooms
**NFL Stadium Standards (Reference Example):**
- **Number of Rooms:** 1 to 7, average 2.4 per stadium
- **Room Size:** 120 to 2,000 square feet, mean 434 square feet
- **Equipment per Room:** Average 3.3 stretchers or tables
- **Telephone:** Present in 91% of all rooms
- **Sinks:** Present in 88% of all rooms

**High and Medium Risk Events:**
- Purpose-specific designed first aid rooms required
- Medical rooms/posts and/or centers
- Properly equipped facilities
- Temporary or permanent emergency back-up generator
- Generator capable of powering essential systems minimum 4 hours

#### Medical Room Equipment
- Examination tables or stretchers
- Medical supply cabinets
- Handwashing sinks
- Adequate lighting
- Climate control
- Privacy curtains or separate rooms
- Communication systems (phone/radio)
- Emergency medical equipment
- Defibrillator (AED)

### 11.2 Ambulance and Emergency Medical Services

#### On-Site Ambulance Requirements
- **Minimum:** All stadiums have at least one ambulance dedicated for spectators
- **NFL Standard:** Range of 1-7 ambulances, mean 2.9 per stadium
- **Positioning:** Strategic placement for rapid access to all areas
- **Access:** Clear routes for emergency vehicle ingress/egress

#### Emergency Vehicle Access
- Designated emergency vehicle routes
- Minimum clear width requirements
- Access to field/playing surface
- Connection to public roads
- No obstacles or impediments

### 11.3 Medical Staffing Requirements

#### Venue Staff Training
- Basic first aid certification required
- CPR certification for designated staff
- AED training and equipment staged throughout venue
- Minimum number of trained staff based on occupancy

#### Advanced Medical Support
- On-site EMT units for larger events
- Physicians for high-risk events
- Full medical suites with diagnostic equipment
- X-ray and ultrasound equipment (major facilities)
- Sports medicine specialists for athlete care

#### Staffing Levels
Based on event type and attendance:
- Community events: Basic first aid trained staff
- Regional competitions: EMT presence
- Professional/major events: Full medical team including physicians

### 11.4 Medical Needs Assessment

#### Assessment Requirements
- Completed to determine appropriate level of medical provision
- Carried out by competent person or medical provider
- Medical Coordinator or Event Doctor should conduct
- Factors to consider:
  - Type of sport/event
  - Expected attendance
  - Duration of event
  - Demographics of attendees
  - Historical incident data
  - Weather conditions
  - Accessibility of external emergency services

### 11.5 Emergency Action Plan (EAP)

#### EAP Components
- Established for each separate venue
- In effect at all practices and competitions
- Documented procedures for medical emergencies
- Communication protocols
- Evacuation procedures
- Integration with local emergency services

#### EAP Documentation Should Include:
- Emergency contact information
- Location of nearest medical facilities
- On-site medical resources
- Chain of command
- Communication systems
- Specific emergency scenarios and responses
- Athlete emergency information
- Venue maps with emergency access points

#### EAP Distribution
- Provided to all staff and coaches
- Reviewed regularly and updated
- Practiced through drills
- Posted in key locations

### 11.6 AED (Automated External Defibrillator) Requirements

#### Placement
- Staged throughout venue for maximum coverage
- Goal: Access within 3-5 minutes from any location
- Clearly marked locations
- Accessible 24/7 during operations

#### Quantity
Based on facility size and occupancy:
- Small facilities: Minimum 1 AED
- Large stadiums: Multiple units strategically placed
- One per major area (gym, pool, field, etc.)

## 12. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

### 12.1 Access Control Systems

#### Purpose and Function
- Control entry to specific areas within sports facility
- Restrict access to authorized individuals only
- Protect restricted areas (locker rooms, VIP areas, equipment storage, control rooms)

#### Access Control Technologies

**Credential-Based Systems:**
- Keycards and proximity cards
- Biometric scanners (fingerprint, facial recognition)
- PIN codes
- Mobile credentials (smartphone-based)
- Multi-factor authentication for high-security areas

**Non-Event Credentialing:**
- Venue staff credentials
- Security personnel access
- Vehicle credentialing
- Contractor and vendor access
- Visitor management systems
- Campus recreation access
- Sport camp attendees

#### Restricted Area Management
Multiple user groups requiring unique access levels:
- Equipment managers
- Sports medicine trainers
- Coaching staff
- Maintenance personnel
- Media and broadcast teams
- VIP and suite access
- Player and athlete areas

### 12.2 Video Surveillance Systems

#### System Requirements

**Camera Coverage:**
- High-definition (HD) cameras for clear footage
- Coverage of all public areas
- Perimeter monitoring including parking lots
- Entrance and exit points
- Restricted area monitoring
- Blind spot elimination

**Monitoring Capabilities:**
- Real-time footage monitoring
- Security personnel oversight
- Crowd behavior observation
- Threat detection and deterrence

## 14. IBC BUILDING CODE REQUIREMENTS

### 14.1 Occupancy Classification

#### Assembly Group A Occupancies
Sports facilities classified under Assembly Group A occupancy per International Building Code (IBC).

**Group A-4: Indoor Sporting Events**
- Arenas
- Skating rinks
- Swimming pools
- Tennis courts
- Indoor sports facilities with spectator seating

**Group A-5: Outdoor Activities**
- Assembly uses for participation in or viewing outdoor activities
- Outdoor stadiums
- Outdoor amphitheaters
- Outdoor sports facilities

#### Occupancy Threshold
- **Assembly Classification:** 50 or more occupants
- **Business Group B:** Fewer than 50 occupants (reclassified from Assembly)
- **Mixed Occupancy:** Facilities may have multiple occupancy classifications

### 14.2 Egress Requirements

#### Exit Requirements for Assembly

**Exit Access Travel Distance:**
- **With NFPA 13/13R Sprinklers:** 250 feet maximum
- **Without Sprinklers:** 200 feet maximum
- Measured from most remote point to entrance of exit

**Common Path of Egress Travel:**
- **Maximum:** 75 feet or less in Assembly occupancies
- Portion of exit access traveled before two separate paths available

**Number of Exits:**
- Based on occupant load
- Areas serving >1,000 people: Minimum 4 remote exits
- Smaller occupancies: Graduated requirements based on load

#### Exit Width and Capacity

**Exit Width Calculations:**
- Level egress: 0.2 inches per occupant (typical for Assembly)
- Stairs: 0.3 inches per occupant
- Minimum 36 inches for exits
- Minimum 44 inches for main exit/exit discharge

### 14.3 Fire Protection Requirements

#### Automatic Sprinkler Systems
Generally required in Assembly Group A occupancies when:
- Fire area exceeds certain square footage thresholds
- Occupant load exceeds specific numbers
- Building height considerations
- Refer to IBC Section 903 for specific requirements

#### Fire Alarm Systems
Required in Group A occupancies:
- Occupant load 300 or more
- Occupant load 100 or more in Group A-4 with smoke-protected seating
- Manual and automatic detection
- Notification appliances throughout

### 14.4 Accessibility Requirements

#### IBC Chapter 11: Accessibility
Sports facilities must comply with accessibility requirements including:
- Accessible routes to all public and common use areas
- Accessible seating (dispersed locations and price ranges)
- Accessible parking
- Accessible toilet and bathing facilities
- Assistive listening systems

#### Assembly Area Accessibility
- Wheelchair spaces and companion seating
- Assistive listening systems required
- Visual alarms in addition to audible
- Accessible routes to performance areas (where provided)

### 14.5 Structural Requirements

#### Live Load Requirements
- Seating areas: 100 psf (pounds per square foot) typical
- Corridors and exitways: 100 psf
- Stages and platforms: 125 psf
- Playing surfaces: Varies by sport and use

#### Special Inspections
- Structural steel
- Concrete
- Seating systems
- Grandstands and bleachers

### 14.6 Special Requirements for Assembly Occupancies

#### Crowd Managers
Required in larger facilities or events where more than 500 people congregate:
- Trained in crowd management
- Emergency procedures knowledge
- Facility evacuation routes
- Communication with emergency services

#### Fire Safety and Evacuation Plan
- Group A occupancies must provide approved plan
- Updated annually
- Training for staff
- Posted information for occupants

- Visualization of accessible routes and circulation patterns

**Performance Optimization:**

- Revolutionized how facilities were designed, built, managed, and operated

- Leading case study for BIM implementation in stadium design

### 15.3 3D Modeling Components for Accessibility

#### Accessible Routes

**Modeling Requirements:**
- Minimum 36-inch clear width paths
- 60 × 60-inch passing spaces
- Proper slopes (1:20 maximum for walking surfaces)
- Connection of all accessible elements
- Door swing clearances
- Turning radii for wheelchairs (60-inch diameter)

**Surface Modeling:**
- Level changes limited to 1/2 inch maximum
- Edge treatments for changes 1/4 to 1/2 inch
- Slip-resistant surface textures
- Tactile warning surface modeling

#### Ramps

**3D Modeling Specifications:**
- Slope: 1:12 maximum (8.33%)
- Width: 36 inches minimum clear
- Maximum rise per run: 30 inches
- Landings: 60 × 60 inches minimum
- Handrails: 34-38 inches height, both sides
- Edge protection: 2-inch minimum height
- Top and bottom landings

#### Accessible Seating Areas

**Modeling Requirements:**
- Wheelchair space: Clear floor space
- Companion seating adjacent to wheelchair spaces
- Distribution throughout seating bowl (horizontal and vertical)
- Sightline analysis from wheelchair positions
- Access to aisles and exits
- Integration with regular seating

**Sightline Calculations:**
- C-value calculations for stepped seating
- Clear view over standing spectators
- Lines of sight to all parts of playing area/stage
- Verification that wheelchair positions not obstructed

#### Elevators and Platform Lifts

**3D Modeling Details:**
- Car dimensions: 51 × 68 inches minimum
- Door clear width: 36 inches minimum
- Call button heights: 48 inches maximum
- Car control heights: 48 inches maximum
- Hoistway and machine room spaces
- Pit and overhead clearances

#### Accessible Parking

**Modeling Elements:**
- Space dimensions: 8 feet (standard) or 11 feet (van)
- Access aisle: 5 feet wide
- Slope: 1:48 maximum
- Accessible route from parking to entrance
- Signage locations (60 inches to bottom of sign)
- Van-accessible identification

## DESIGN CHECKLIST SUMMARY

### ADA Compliance
- [ ] Accessible routes connecting all public areas (36" minimum width)
- [ ] Wheelchair seating: Minimum 1% of total seating
- [ ] Companion seats adjacent to all wheelchair spaces
- [ ] Wheelchair seating dispersed horizontally and vertically
- [ ] Accessible parking: Proper number and van-accessible spaces
- [ ] Accessible locker rooms: 5% minimum, proper bench specifications
- [ ] Accessible restrooms: Grab bars, turning space, proper fixture heights
- [ ] Ramps: 1:12 maximum slope, 60" landings, handrails
- [ ] Elevators: 51 × 68" car, 36" door width, proper controls

- [ ] Equitable use for all abilities
- [ ] Flexibility in use and approach
- [ ] Simple and intuitive wayfinding
- [ ] Perceptible information (visual and tactile)
- [ ] Tolerance for error in design
- [ ] Low physical effort required
- [ ] Appropriate size and space for approach and use

- [ ] Fire alarm system with voice evacuation
- [ ] Automatic sprinkler system throughout
- [ ] Emergency lighting: 90-minute duration minimum
- [ ] Exit signage: Illuminated, visible from all directions
- [ ] Proper number of exits based on occupant load
- [ ] Exit travel distance within code limits
- [ ] First aid rooms properly equipped
- [ ] AEDs staged throughout facility
- [ ] Emergency Action Plan documented and practiced

### Security
- [ ] Access control system for restricted areas
- [ ] Video surveillance covering all public areas
- [ ] Perimeter security and lighting
- [ ] Entry screening procedures
- [ ] Integration of security systems
